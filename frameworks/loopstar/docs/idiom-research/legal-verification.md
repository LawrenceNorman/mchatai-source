# Adversarial Fact-Check: Music-Copyright Report for LoopStar Phrase Packs

Verified via web search, Aug 2026. Verdicts on each load-bearing claim, then the corrected design-rules checklist. Not legal advice.

## 1. Case-outcome verdicts

### 1.1 Dark Horse appeal — *Gray v. Hudson*, 28 F.4th 87 (9th Cir. 2022) — **CONFIRMED**
- Decided Mar. 10, 2022. Jury had awarded $2.8M; district court granted JMOL and vacated; 9th Cir. affirmed. Held the 8-note "Joyful Noise" ostinato was built from commonplace, unoriginal musical elements not protectable by copyright; a combination of unprotectable elements gets at most thin protection. Citation (28 F.4th 87) verified via Google Scholar. (Justia; Fenwick; Law.com.)

### 1.2 Ed Sheeran 2023 — *Griffin v. Sheeran* (S.D.N.Y. jury) — **CONFIRMED**
- Verdict May 4, 2023 (report says "May 2023" — correct). Unanimous jury found "Thinking Out Loud" did not infringe "Let's Get It On"; Sheeran independently created it. Plaintiffs were heirs of co-writer Ed Townsend. (Rolling Stone; ABC; PBS.)

### 1.3 *Structured Asset Sales v. Sheeran*, 120 F.4th 1066 (2d Cir. Nov. 1, 2024) — **CONFIRMED**
- Decided Nov. 1, 2024. Affirmed judgment as a matter of law for Sheeran (technically affirmance of summary judgment, not a motion-to-dismiss — immaterial nuance; report's "as a matter of law" is accurate). Quoted holding verified nearly verbatim: the four-chord progression, "ubiquitous in pop music," "even coupled with a syncopated harmonic rhythm, is too well explored to meet the originality threshold"; selection-and-arrangement combination also unprotectable. 1909-Act deposit-copy rule confirmed: scope limited to the 1973 deposited sheet music; bass line from the recording excluded. Detail: court noted Townsend used I–iii–IV–V, Sheeran I–I–IV–V — similar but not identical progressions. (Justia; Loeb; IPWatchdog; Snell & Wilmer.)

### 1.4 Stairway — *Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin*, 952 F.3d 1051 (9th Cir. en banc 2020) — **CONFIRMED**
- Decided Mar. 9, 2020, en banc. Affirmed the non-infringement jury verdict; unanimously **abolished the inverse-ratio rule** (joining four other circuits); confirmed 1909-Act works are defined by the deposit copy (Taurus = one page); addressed originality and selection-and-arrangement instructions consistent with the report's characterization. (WilmerHale; NatLawReview; FKKS.)

### 1.5 Blurred Lines — *Williams v. Gaye*, 895 F.3d 1106 (9th Cir. 2018) — **CONFIRMED**
- 9th Cir. affirmed the infringement verdict expressly "on narrow grounds," leaning on procedural posture/deference to the jury, giving "virtually no guidance" on inspiration-vs-infringement. Judge Nguyen's dissent — that the majority let the Gaye estate "copyright a musical style"/groove and dealt "a devastating blow to future musicians" — confirmed and is indeed the most-discussed part. The report's framing (judgment settled, reasoning contested, later confined by *Skidmore*/*Gray*) is accurate. (Justia; Jones Day; Haynes Boone; NYU JIPEL.)

### 1.6 Under Pressure / Ice Ice Baby — **CONFIRMED, one flag**
- Confirmed: **no court ruling exists**; Queen/Bowie threatened suit; matter settled privately out of court with money and songwriting credits. Report correctly labels it industry folklore, not precedent.
- **FLAG (soften, don't rely):** "he later bought the publishing" is Vanilla Ice's **own interview claim** (~$4M), widely repeated (Exclaim!: "Apparently Vanilla Ice Owns the Rights") but never independently verified and sometimes questioned. Downgrade from asserted fact to "Vanilla Ice claims." Does not affect any design rule. (GWU MCIR; American Songwriter; Exclaim!.)

## 2. US public-domain cutoff as of 2026 — **CONFIRMED**
- Compositions **published 1930 and earlier are US-PD** as of Jan. 1, 2026 (Duke CSPD Public Domain Day 2026; Copyright Lately). 1931 arrives Jan. 1, 2027.
- Named 1930 works confirmed PD: "I Got Rhythm," "Georgia on My Mind," "Dream a Little Dream of Me."
- Sound recordings under the MMA: **pre-1923 PD in 2022; 1925 recordings PD Jan. 1, 2026** (1923–1946 = 100-year term; 1947–1956 = 110; 1957–2/15/1972 protected to 2067). Report's schedule matches Copyright Office/CRS/UCSB ADP sources exactly.
- EU caveat (life+70; Ira Gershwin d. 1983 lyrics still protected in EU) is consistent with the term rules; not independently re-verified per-work but structurally correct.

## 3. Chord progressions unprotectable — **CONFIRMED**
- Multiple doctrinal sources (LegalClarity; Local 802 AFM; GWU MCIR) + *SAS v. Sheeran* (2d Cir. 2024) confirm progressions are common-stock building blocks, unprotectable even combined with syncopated harmonic rhythm.
- **Nuance to keep (report already flags it in §1.2):** "verbatim, at unlimited length" is an extrapolation, not a tested holding for an extremely long/idiosyncratic through-composed sequence; the report's own LIKELY-labeled selection-and-arrangement tail is the honest statement. No checklist change needed — rule 1 is fine for degree-level genre loops.

## 4. Prior-art characterizations — **MOSTLY CONFIRMED, two corrections**
- **Arranger keyboards / Band-in-a-Box**: no infringement suit against pattern vendors located in searches (negative claim — absence of evidence, consistent with report). Toontrack EZkeys packs confirmed marketed by genre ("Americana," "Epic Metal," "Hooks & Chords"), "inspired by," performed by session players, MIDI-only, **no artist names in pack titles** — confirmed from Toontrack product pages. (EULA redistribution-ban detail: standard and plausible, not directly verified.)
- **CORRECTION (minor) — Yamaha v. Deli**: Suit filed **Nov. 2006** (Beijing Second Intermediate People's Court), resolved **2008**. The court formally recognized Yamaha's copyright in only **26 of the 425** asserted style datasets; the rest resolved by settlement in which defendants acknowledged Yamaha's copyright and paid ¥110,000. So "style banks are protectable content" is supported but rests partly on settlement, not a clean 425-style holding. **Strengthener the report missed:** *Yamaha v. Medeli* (High Court of Hong Kong, judgment Jan. 2022, announced Oct. 2023) — judgment for Yamaha on copied Style Data preset in 46 keyboard models; USD 850,000 damages+costs. Style-data-as-copyrightable now has two wins. (Japan Today; archive.yamaha.com 2008 release; yamaha.com 2023 release.)
- **CORRECTION (minor) — Norton Music naming**: The report says third-party style vendors use "punning/coded song references and disclaimers rather than straight titles." Norton's site openly markets styles **"in the style of [artist]"** using real band names (e.g., Average White Band, Weather Report) — i.e., artist names DO appear in third-party marketing, presumably as claimed nominative fair use; the pun/coded convention applies to *song-title*-indexed content. So industry practice on artist names is less uniform than stated — Toontrack omits names; Norton uses them with "in the style of" framing. The report's conservative design rule (omit artist names) remains the right call; only the factual characterization needed correcting. (nortonmusic.com; disclaimer text could not be fetched — cert error — so the "disclaimers" claim is unverified.)
- **Hooktheory** — **CONFIRMED**: self-imposed fair-use posture is real and published — analyses "generally not exceeding 30 seconds or 10% of the song's length, whichever is shorter," no harmonically redundant sections, educational framing; DMCA policy with repeat-infringer termination; TOS bans scraping/bulk export of the TheoryTab DB (~50k transcriptions). Untested-in-court characterization stands. The "takedowns hit embedded YouTube videos, not note data" detail: **unverified** (plausible; treat as anecdote). (hooktheory.com/dmca; hooktheory.com/terms.)

## 5. Citation errata (non-substantive)
- **Newton v. Diamond**: 388 F.3d 1189 is the **2004 amended opinion**; the 2003 original is 349 F.3d 591. Cite as *Newton v. Diamond*, 388 F.3d 1189 (9th Cir. 2004). Holding (3-note/6-second figure de minimis at composition level) unchanged.
- **Pelham II**: confirmed real and correctly dated — CJEU C-590/23, decided **Apr. 14, 2026**, clarifying the pastiche exception for recognizable-but-transformed sampling. (Jones Day; Greenberg Traurig; Bird & Bird.)

## 6. Corrected design-rules checklist (changes marked ★; all others re-affirmed as written)

**A. May be copied verbatim from any source, including in-copyright classics:**
1. Chord progressions, degree-level or absolute, any normal loop length — incl. a specific song's changes. (★ Retain the report's own §1.2 caveat: an extraordinarily long/unusual through-composed harmonic sequence is untested; genre loops are unambiguous.)
2. Harmonic-rhythm placements, even combined with the progression (*SAS v. Sheeran*, confirmed verbatim).
3. Rhythm archetypes: grooves, clave/skank/dembow/boom-bap cells, swing ratios, standard bass figurations.
4. Voicing techniques (drop-2, rootless, shells, inversions).
5. Instrumentation/GM choices, tempo, densities, forms.
6. PD melodies/bass lines: compositions **published ≤1930 (US)** from period editions, with `pdSource` field. (Cutoff confirmed for 2026; bump to ≤1931 on Jan. 1, 2027.)

**B. Must be ORIGINAL-IN-IDIOM:**
7. Melodic hooks/riffs/ostinatos: author fresh cells from genre vocabulary; no transcribed hooks regardless of length (*Gray* makes tiny commonplace figures safe, but the zero-transcription policy is cheap insurance — re-affirmed).
8. Named-song bass lines: style-alike technique, never the note sequence. (★ Under Pressure basis re-labeled: private settlement, no precedent; "Ice bought the publishing" is his unverified claim — the rule stands on melody doctrine, not that anecdote.)
9. No single-song full-texture clones (Blurred Lines constellation tail — confirmed narrow/jury-deference holding, Nguyen dissent, confined by *Skidmore*/*Gray* but never overruled).
10. Musicologist test: "sounds like the genre" = goal; "recognizably that song" = bug.

**C. Provenance & process:**
11. Per-phrase authorship records; your original phrases are protectable content you own. (★ Basis strengthened: Yamaha's Beijing 2008 win — 26 styles adjudicated + settlement — **plus** Yamaha v. Medeli, HK High Court 2022, USD 850k. Two wins, not one.)
12. PD transcriptions from pre-1931 imprints only (arrangement trap, Circular 14 — re-affirmed).
13. If user submissions ever ship: Hooktheory kit (TOS + DMCA agent + takedown + repeat-infringer policy) — confirmed as Hooktheory's actual published posture; remember it is an untested fair-use position, so for user-submitted transcriptions of in-copyright songs you'd be adopting a defense, not a safe harbor from composition liability itself (DMCA §512 helps against hosting liability only).
14–17. Naming/marketing rules re-affirmed. (★ One factual amendment to the rationale, not the rule: industry practice is split — Toontrack omits artist names; Norton openly sells "in the style of [artist]" styles as nominative fair use. Follow the Toontrack norm anyway: it costs nothing and eliminates Lanham/right-of-publicity surface. Titles-not-copyrightable and nominative-fair-use bases confirmed.)
18. Own-synthesis rendering: §114(b) sound-alike carve-out is statutory text; no third-party sound-recording exposure when audio derives from your own note data + CC0/licensed SoundFonts. Keep SoundFont provenance — re-affirmed.

**Bottom line (unchanged after checking):** every SETTLED-labeled case claim checked out, including direct quotes; the two danger zones (verbatim in-copyright hooks/bass lines; artist-name marketing) and the green zones (progressions, grooves, styles, PD-≤1930, own-synth rendering) survive adversarial review. Corrections were confined to: Vanilla Ice publishing purchase (unverified claim), Yamaha v. Deli scope (26/425 + settlement; add Medeli 2022), Norton naming practice (artist names are used openly), and the Newton citation year (2004).
