# Song Book candidates — the modern-relevant backlog

The v1 corpus reads old-timey for one reason: in the US, public domain means
**published before 1931**. That is a hard wall, not a taste.

But "old" and "irrelevant to modern music" are very different things, and the gap
between them is where the best additions live. Four angles, best first.

**Before encoding anything from this list, verify its publication year.** The
years below are the working assumption, and the ones sitting within a year or two
of the 1931 line are exactly where being wrong matters. The validator checks the
claim against the stated basis; it cannot check the year itself.

## 2. The forms modern music is actually built on

1930 rolled into the public domain on 1 January 2026, and it is a very good year.

| Song | Why it matters | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **I Got Rhythm** | **Rhythm changes** — the second most-used form in jazz after the blues, and the backbone of bebop. One entry buys a whole vocabulary. | Gershwin, 1930 |
| **Georgia on My Mind** | Hoagy Carmichael, 1930 — Ray Charles' signature | 1930 |
| **Body and Soul** | the most-recorded jazz ballad ever written | Johnny Green, 1930 |
| **Star Dust** | Carmichael; the instrumental predates the lyric | 1927 |
| **On the Sunny Side of the Street** | McHugh & Fields | 1930 |
| **Embraceable You**, **But Not for Me** | Gershwin standards | 1930 |
| **The Peanut Vendor** (*El Manisero*) | the record that started the Latin-jazz craze — the son clave enters American pop here | Moisés Simons, 1930 |
| **Puttin' on the Ritz** | Berlin; Taco's 1983 synth-pop hit is this song | 1929 |
| **Happy Birthday to You** | melody is "Good Morning to All" (1893); the lyrics were ruled public domain in 2016 | free, finally |
| **Take Me Out to the Ball Game** | Norworth & Von Tilzer | 1908 |

⚠️ These are mostly **32-bar AABA** with **two chords per bar** in places. v1 is
one-chord-per-bar, so a ii-V inside a bar has to be simplified. They will land,
but they are the strongest argument for doing sub-bar changes next.

### ⚠️ Early jazz needs a score, not recall — measured, not assumed

The v1 build attempted eight early-jazz and blues heads (St. Louis Blues, Sweet
Georgia Brown, After You've Gone, Some of These Days, Basin Street Blues, Royal
Garden Blues, Avalon, The Parting Glass). **Every one was refused at encode
time**, each for the same reason: the form and the changes were certain, but
note-level melodic recall was not, and the rule is that an invented melody
attributed to a real composition is worse than shipping nothing.

That is a real finding about this repertoire, not a one-off. Traditional and
folk material survives as a *tune* people carry; an early-jazz head survives as a
*record*, and its melody is far less reliably reconstructable from memory. The
blues entries that did land (Corrina Corrina, Stagger Lee, St. James Infirmary,
Nobody Knows You) are all traditional-lineage songs, not composed heads.

**So this whole category needs a different pipeline: transcribe from an actual
public-domain score or lead sheet, rather than asking a model to recall it.**
Until then, do not re-attempt them the same way — the failure is expected and
the refusals were correct.

## 4. What v1 cannot reach yet — and what unblocking costs

**Compound and triple meter is the single biggest lock on this corpus.** Behind
it, all genuinely public domain and all first-rank:

> Amazing Grace · Greensleeves · House of the Rising Sun · Scarborough Fair ·
> Gymnopédie No. 1 · Clair de Lune · Für Elise · Auld Lang Syne (some settings) ·
> Cielito Lindo · Danny Boy in its 3/4 settings · every Irish jig · every waltz

That is not a long tail — it is a second corpus of comparable quality to the
first. If the Song Book is worth extending, **3/4 and 6/8 support buys more than
any number of additional 4/4 songs will**, and it is an engine change (the bar is
four beats everywhere), not a content one.

Second priority is **sub-bar chord changes**, which is what unlocks section 2's
jazz standards at full fidelity rather than simplified.

## 5. The rolling frontier — a free content drop every January

One more year enters the public domain each 1 January:

- **Jan 2027 → 1931:** "Minnie the Moocher", "Mood Indigo", "Dream a Little Dream
  of Me", "As Time Goes By"
- **Jan 2028 → 1932:** "It Don't Mean a Thing (If It Ain't Got That Swing)",
  "Night and Day"
- **Jan 2029 → 1933:** "Sophisticated Lady", "Smoke Gets in Your Eyes"

Worth a recurring reminder: the Song Book gets meaningfully better every year for
free, and the newly-freed material is exactly the swing-era repertoire that reads
as least old-timey.

## 6. Do NOT encode these — commonly assumed free, and not

| Song | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Guantanamera** | Melody credited to José Fernández Díaz (d. 1979). Not PD. |
| **Summertime** | Gershwin, 1935. Not until 2031. |
| **The Lion Sleeps Tonight** / *Mbube* | Solomon Linda, 1939. Not PD, and the rights history is notorious. |
| **This Land Is Your Land** | Guthrie, 1940/45. Disputed but not free. |
| **We Shall Overcome** | Litigated 2015-18; partly freed, still messy. Avoid. |
| **Cotton Fields** | A Lead Belly composition (1940), not a traditional. |
| **Kumbaya** | Traditional in practice, but with live authorship claims. |

And a distinction that catches people constantly: for **House of the Rising Sun**,
**Wellerman**, **You Gotta Move** and many folk revivals, the *song* is
traditional and free while the *famous arrangement* is a named person's
copyrighted work. Encode the traditional version and say so in `pd.notes` — never
transcribe the record.
