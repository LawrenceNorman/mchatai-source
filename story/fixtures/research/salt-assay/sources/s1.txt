On the weighing of salt. A merchant who would know the true weight of a cart of rock salt must first let it dry, for wet salt may carry a tenth part of its weight in water. The assayer breaks a sample from three places in the load, grinds it, and weighs it before and after it is warmed on an iron plate. The loss is the water. A tally that records the wet weight will always overstate the salt.
